Why this task exists

We build AI products for businesses. The job is not "write code to spec" — it's take a fuzzy business
problem, decide what the product should be, and ship something a real customer could use.

This task is deliberately under-specified. Part of what we're grading is the decisions you make when
nobody tells you what to do.

You are expected to use AI tools (Claude Code, Cursor, whatever you use). That's the job. We're not
testing whether you can write a for-loop from memory.

The problem

Search is moving from Google to AI. People now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI
Overviews instead of typing keywords into a search bar.

That breaks the entire SEO industry's playbook. A business can rank #1 on Google and be completely
invisible inside AI answers, because AI engines choose their sources by a different set of rules — and
most businesses have no idea what those rules are.

This new discipline is called GEO — Generative Engine Optimization (also called AEO / LLMO / AI
SEO).

Right now, almost no business knows whether they're visible in AI search. There is no easy way to
check. That's the gap.

What to build

A GEO Auditor.

A user enters a business — at minimum a website URL — and gets back an audit report that tells them:

1
 How visible they currently are in AI search (a score, and the evidence behind it)

2
 What is broken (specific, with proof — not generic advice)

3
 What to fix, in what order (prioritized by impact vs effort)

That's it. How you build it, what stack, what the report looks like — your call.

What to actually check — you decide

We are not giving you a checklist. Figuring out what to measure is the task.

Nobody has agreed on what a GEO audit should contain yet. The field is about two years old. So go
research it: read what's being written about AI search visibility, look at what the early tools in this space
do, poke at a few AI engines yourself and notice what makes them cite one source over another. Then
decide what belongs in your auditor.

Two rules:

•
 Go deep, not wide. Three checks done properly beat twelve that tick boxes. A narrow tool that works
beats a wide tool that half-works.

•
 Be able to defend every check. In your README, tell us why each one is in there and why the ones
you skipped aren't. "It was easy to build" is an acceptable reason if you say so honestly.

If two candidates submit tools that check completely different things, that's expected. We're reading your
reasoning as much as your code.

The report is the product

Read this section twice. The report is what we're actually evaluating. A beautiful scraper with an ugly
report scores badly.

Requirements:

•
 A score with a visible breakdown. If you show a number, we must be able to see how it was
calculated. No magic 73/100.

•
 Every finding must carry evidence. A vague "your site isn't optimized" is worthless. Name the exact
page, show what you found, and show what it should be instead.

•
 Prioritized fixes, ordered by impact x effort. The business owner should know what to do Monday
morning.

•
 Written for a business owner, not an SEO consultant. If a fix needs jargon, explain the jargon
inline.

•
 Copy-pasteable output wherever the fix allows it. Don't describe the fix — hand it to them.

Format is your choice: web page, PDF, dashboard, whatever you can defend.

What we are NOT asking for

•
 Auth, signup, billing, user accounts — skip entirely

•
 A database, unless you actually need one

 Questions are welcome — ask any time, it's a positive signal. But don't wait on an answer to start.

One last thing

Build for that moment.
